What happens to your privacy when robots go to work?

Figure AI just deployed Helix 02, a humanoid robot that works alongside humans in warehouses, watching, learning, and collecting data 24/7. Nobody asked the workers if that was okay.

This blog is a research project exploring what that actually means for your privacy. We're four students — Eden, Orianne, Dan & Shir — who spent a semester digging into the legal gaps, the ethical questions, and what regulations (if any) can keep up with this technology.

Eden Ledom@edenledom·2hStrategy
A semester of research led us here. This is the governance framework humanoid robots actually need. The law isn't keeping up. So we built a model that does. • Tier 1: Privacy by design baked into the hardware, before it hits the market. Not a checkbox. Architecture. • Tier 2: A data protection impact assessment required per sector, before any deployment. • Tier 3: Functional and training data treated as legally distinct, because they serve completely different purposes. • Tier 4: GDPR as the global floor, with US sector-specific rules layered on top. This is the gap between where the law is and where it has to be. @oriblum @danvered @shirschipper

SS
Shir Schipper@shirschipper·4dEthics
Nobody talks about the gap between ethics and engineering in robotics nearly enough. ACM's Code of Ethics has had a simple directive since 1992: respect privacy. That principle hasn't changed. But the architecture it was written for looks nothing like what we're deploying today. A humanoid robot with full-body continuous biometric sensing isn't a desktop computer. It's something else entirely. The principle still holds. The architecture doesn't. Nobody has resolved that tension yet.

DV
Dan Vered@danvered·5dStrategy
Separating functional data from training data isn't just a technical distinction, it's the most urgent regulatory gap in embodied AI right now. Without it, everything defaults to product telemetry law. A framework built for faulty brake pads. Not for robots that observe, remember, and learn from your behavior. Without this legal separation: • Behavioral profiling gets treated like sensor diagnostics • There's no meaningful limit on how long training data can be kept • Developers have little incentive to draw the line themselves The law needs to catch up, and it needs to start with the right definitions.
